RyoGas: reliable sales workflows for fuel stations

I helped turn a manual fuel station process into a mobile-first platform for sales tracking, reporting, and day-to-day operations, including locations where internet access is unreliable.

01 / Context

The operating problem came before the software.

Fuel stations often record sales through manual steps. That makes reporting slower, increases the chance of data-entry mistakes, and gives owners less visibility into daily performance. The product also had to work for busy, non-technical users and remain useful when connectivity dropped.

RyoGas began as a university startup idea and became an operating technology company. I started as lead developer, then moved into the co-founder and CEO role while continuing to shape the product and its technical direction.

02 / Approach

Map the real workflow, then remove friction.

I translated the station's manual sales and reporting process into structured mobile workflows. The interface reduced unnecessary steps and kept daily tasks clear for people who were not accustomed to business software.

  • Offline-first operation: core workflows continued in low-connectivity environments.
  • Cross-platform delivery: the mobile application used Flutter, Dart, Java, and Firebase services.
  • Operational reporting: sales activity moved into consistent digital records that could support recurring reporting.
  • Product discovery: technical decisions followed the users' daily process instead of assuming ideal conditions.

04 / Product lesson

Reliability is part of the user experience.

This project shaped how I approach data and AI systems now. A technically sound model or pipeline still has to fit the decision, workflow, and operating conditions around it. When those constraints are understood early, the product becomes easier to use and easier to trust.
